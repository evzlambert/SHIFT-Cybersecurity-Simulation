# SHIFT Cybersecurity Breach Simulation — Assignment

**Points:** 200  
**Submission:** Team export (JSON) submitted via Canvas + individual after-action reflection submitted separately  
**CAHIIM Domains:** I (Data Governance), II (Information Protection), V (Health Law & Compliance), VI (Organizational Management)

---

## Overview

This assignment assesses your performance across the full SHIFT Cybersecurity Breach Simulation. You will be evaluated on five competency areas that mirror the real responsibilities of a Health Information Management professional during and after a large-scale ransomware event. Your grade reflects the quality of your team's decisions, your hands-on documentation deliverables, your regulatory response, your crisis communications, and your individual capacity for critical reflection.

---

## Submission Instructions

1. **Team Export:** At the end of the simulation, one team member clicks **Export Team Report** in the student app. This automatically saves your team's data to the instructor dashboard. Confirm the success message appears before leaving.
2. **Individual Reflection:** Each student submits the After-Action Summary (Questions 1–5) individually via Canvas in their own words, even though the discussion happened as a team.
3. **Due Date:** Submitted by end of class on simulation day. No late submissions accepted — this is a live-participation activity.

## Rubric

### Criterion 1: Downtime Documentation and Data Governance
**Points: 60 | CAHIIM Outcomes: PI 1.1.1, PI 1.1.2, PI 1.3.1**

*Evaluates the quality of paper-based documentation artifacts designed during downtime: the emergency MAR, patient ID wristband, shift handoff tool, and paper patient intake record. Focuses on whether essential data elements are present, whether patient safety is prioritized, and whether the team demonstrates understanding of health record content standards.*

| Rating | Points | Description |
|--------|--------|-------------|
| Exceeds Mastery | 60 | All downtime documentation artifacts are complete and demonstrate exceptional understanding of health record data elements, patient safety requirements, and documentation standards. The MAR includes robust safety features (allergy flagging, dual-signature, second identifier). The wristband solves the dual-patient problem with a clear, durable solution. The handoff tool is comprehensive and includes downtime-specific elements beyond a standard SBAR. The paper intake record captures all required clinical elements. Peer review feedback is substantive and specific. |
| Mastery | 45 | Downtime documentation artifacts are complete and demonstrate solid understanding of health record content requirements. Safety features are present and appropriate. Wristband addresses the identification challenge. Handoff tool covers key patient and operational information. Paper record captures essential elements; peer review identifies meaningful gaps. |
| Near Mastery | 30 | Most artifacts are completed but one or more are missing key data elements or safety considerations. Safety features are present but basic. Identification solution is functional but incomplete. Handoff tool or paper record omits important categories. Peer review is brief. |
| Below Mastery | 15 | Artifacts are incomplete or reflect minimal understanding of documentation requirements. Safety features are absent or inadequate. Patient identification not meaningfully addressed. One or more artifacts not completed during the simulation. |
| Not Evident | 0 | No downtime documentation artifacts submitted or team did not participate in action stations. |

*Evaluates the quality and justification of the team's decisions across all four active phases (The Breach, Downtime, Escalation, Critical Decisions). Focuses on whether decisions reflect understanding of cybersecurity threats to health information, incident command principles, and the role of HIM in an organizational crisis.*

| Rating | Points | Description |
|--------|--------|-------------|

Board recommendation memo articulates a defensible position with clear rationale and acknowledgment of dissenting views. |
| Mastery | 36 | Decisions are well-reasoned with justifications that reflect understanding of the cybersecurity context and organizational responsibilities. Trade-offs are acknowledged. Board recommendation is clear and supported by logical reasoning. |
| Near Mastery | 24 | Most decisions include justification but reasoning is surface-level or inconsistent across phases. Some decisions appear reactive without clear rationale. Board recommendation is present but lacks depth or ignores key considerations. |

### Criterion 3: Regulatory Compliance and Legal Response
**Points: 40 | CAHIIM Outcomes: PI 2.2.1, PI 5.1.1, PI 5.2.1, PI 5.3.1**

*Evaluates the team's CNIL breach notification (GDPR Article 33) and GDPR-related decisions. Focuses on whether the team correctly identifies regulatory obligations, accurately represents what is known and unknown, and demonstrates understanding of the legal and ethical dimensions of breach disclosure in a healthcare context.*

| Rating | Points | Description |
|--------|--------|-------------|
| Exceeds Mastery | 40 | CNIL notification is complete, accurate, and legally defensible. All required fields are addressed. The team clearly distinguishes confirmed facts from unknowns and explicitly lists gaps — demonstrating understanding that incomplete disclosure with honest unknowns is legally preferable to speculation. Regulatory decisions (GDPR timing, notification scope) reflect understanding of Article 33 obligations and the 72-hour clock. Legal and ethical tensions (patient notification, ransom) are directly addressed. |
| Mastery | 30 | CNIL notification is substantially complete with most required fields addressed. Known and unknown information is differentiated. Regulatory decisions reflect understanding of GDPR obligations. Legal/ethical considerations are acknowledged. |
| Near Mastery | 20 | CNIL notification is partially completed; key fields are missing or vague. The distinction between known and unknown information is not clearly maintained. Regulatory decisions are made without explicit reference to GDPR requirements. |
| Below Mastery | 10 | CNIL notification is largely incomplete or contains significant inaccuracies. Regulatory obligations are not reflected in decision-making. Legal and ethical considerations are absent. |
| Not Evident | 0 | CNIL notification not submitted or GDPR-related decisions not completed. |

### Criterion 5: Recovery Planning and Critical Reflection
**Points: 20 | CAHIIM Outcomes: PI 1.2.1, PI 1.4.1, PI 6.3.1, PI 6.9.3**

*Evaluates the back-entry workflow design and the individual after-action summary. Focuses on whether the team demonstrates understanding of data integrity challenges in recovery, and whether the individual student can critically analyze their own decision-making, identify learning gaps, and connect simulation experiences to professional competencies.*

| Rating | Points | Description |
|--------|--------|-------------|
| Exceeds Mastery | 20 | Back-entry workflow is systematic and safety-prioritized, with a clear plan for unmatched records, QA checkpoints, and realistic staffing considerations. The after-action summary demonstrates genuine critical reflection: the student identifies a specific decision they would change and articulates why, names concrete things learned about cybersecurity in healthcare, and offers specific and actionable preparedness recommendations. Responses go beyond restating what happened to analyzing implications. |
| Mastery | 15 | Back-entry workflow addresses priority, process steps, QA, and the unmatched record problem, even if detail is uneven. After-action responses are thoughtful and specific; the student demonstrates learning and can articulate what they would do differently. |
| Near Mastery | 10 | Back-entry workflow is partially developed; some key elements (QA, unmatched records, staffing) are absent or vague. After-action responses are completed but are generic, brief, or primarily descriptive rather than analytical. |
| Below Mastery | 5 | Back-entry workflow is minimal or impractical. After-action responses are one or two sentences with little evidence of reflection or learning. |
| Not Evident | 0 | Back-entry workflow not completed and/or after-action summary not submitted. |
